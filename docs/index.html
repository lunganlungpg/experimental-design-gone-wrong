    Case 01

    The Lazy Randomization

    “I sprayed the first 20 pots because they were closer to the door.” — every first-year, ever

    BioBoost‑X™ (sprayed) Control (tap water)
    How do we assign pots?

    🕵️ What actually happened

    The pots near the sunny door were always going to be bigger. By spraying those pots you perfectly welded “treatment” to “sunshine”. The t-test can't tell them apart, so it hands the sun's credit to your product.

    Fix: randomize the allocation and put bench_pos in the model (ANCOVA). Flip to 🎲 above and watch the miracle evaporate.

    Case 02

    Blocking the Wrong Way

    “I blocked! I'm a responsible scientist!” — narrator: the blocks ran along the gradient.

    CControlBBiochar KCompostNNanoKelp background = soil moisture
    Which way do the blocks run?

    🕵️ What actually happened

    A block is only useful if the plots inside it are alike. Row‑blocks each contain the whole wet‑to‑dry range, so they soak up none of the gradient. All that moisture variation stays in your error term, your test goes half‑blind, and one unlucky shuffle can still fake a winner.

    Fix: run blocks across the gradient (columns here), or block on both rows and columns. Compare the residual noise (MSE) between the two layouts. Same field, same effort, very different noise.

    And the 😈 button? That's how you find a “significant” seed: keep re-rolling until one works. Please don't.

    Case 03

    The Pseudoreplication Delusion

    “n = 100!” (It's four plants. It has always been four plants.)

    Control plants Fertilized plants leaf size = measured leaf area

    🕵️ What actually happened

    Leaves on the same plant share genes, roots, pot and windowsill. They are not independent experiments; they are the same experiment asked 25 times. Pulling more leaves makes the naive df balloon while the information barely moves. Try 50 leaves and watch the false-positive rate climb toward ridiculous.

    Fix: analyse at the level you randomized (the plant): average per plant, or fit a mixed model with groups=plant_id. Want more power? Buy more plants.

    Case 04

    Split-Plot Amnesia

    “The irrigation was applied to whole strips but I analysed it per subplot because the software let me.”

    Irrigated strip Rainfed strip subplot shade = yield

    🕵️ What actually happened

    Irrigation was randomized to only 10 strips, so irrigation has only 10 experimental units, not 50. The flat ANOVA tests it against the tiny subplot noise (Error b, 40 df) instead of the big strip-to-strip noise (Error a, 4 df). That's how a hose becomes a miracle.

    Fix: test whole-plot factors against whole-plot error: a split-plot ANOVA or MixedLM with a random effect for whole_plot_id. Slide σa to 0 and the two analyses agree. That's the whole lesson in one slider.
